# The Trapwright
### An original class for 2024 D&D • Pacts & Polyhedrals • Playtest 0.1

A Trapwright reads a room the way a duelist reads a stance: where enemies will step, what they'll rush to defend, and which route they'll think is safe. You carry small surprises, not a dungeon in your pockets. Your devices work best when the party persuades enemies to take the route you expected.

The class uses Trap Forge's original fantasy presets as schematics. These are game mechanics, not instructions for building real devices. The Trapwright has no Spellcasting feature. Even the Sigil Binder uses prepared wards rather than spell slots.

## Class Features

### Level 1: Trapcraft

**Charges.** You have a maximum number of Trap Charges equal to twice your Proficiency Bonus. You regain all expended charges when you finish a Long Rest. A portable trap costs one charge. Missing or misplacing it does not return the charge. Neither does dismantling it, unless Last Reserve says otherwise. A rejected placement request never costs a charge.

**Deploy Trap.** As an Action, spend one Trap Charge to place one portable trap from a schematic you have unlocked. Choose an unoccupied, supported 5-foot space you can see within 5 feet of you, on your current floor. Place only one via this feature on a turn. It is armed immediately after placement; placing a device does not itself trigger it. You can't place it inside a solid object, on top of another portable trap, in midair, or beyond the encounter boundary the DM set. Unless a schematic or the DM specifies otherwise, it is hidden from opponents until found or triggered. Friends can pass safely, but do not automatically learn its exact location; you can point it out normally. Use the DM’s ruling for uncertain allegiance, a disguise, or a creature that is neither friend nor foe.

**Number and lifetime.** You may have at most your Proficiency Bonus in armed portable traps at one time. The cap is yours, not per scene, per token, or per subclass. To place another when you are at the cap, first dismantle an old one or wait for it to expire or be spent. A portable trap stops functioning after it triggers once, after 1 hour, when you die, or at the end of your next Long Rest, whichever comes first. Taking down or deleting an expired tile is housekeeping, not refunded resources. Spent artwork can remain on the map as a marker; it isn't an independent hazard. You can take an Action to dismantle one of your own armed traps within 5 feet without triggering it.

**Crossing.** The first opposing creature that enters or crosses the armed square triggers it. Resolve only against that creature, even if the art shows a blast extending farther. Portable traps do not trigger from involuntary movement, being carried, being placed into a square, or teleporting through one. A creature that arrives by teleportation doesn't cross a tile; wait for a later physical crossing. A creature can be affected by at most one portable trap in a single movement, even if it crosses several. Resolve ties in the order the creature encountered them; the others remain armed. Nothing a portable trap does can trigger a second portable trap. These safeguards apply even outside initiative.

**Save.** If there is a save, the portable trap's DC is **8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Intelligence modifier**. For a damaging schematic, use its listed saving throw and damage type. The target takes the rolled damage on a failed save or half that damage (round down) on a successful one, before applying defenses normally. For a damaging schematic with no listed save, use a Dexterity save unless the DM approves a more suitable ability. If a utility schematic calls for a save to resist its listed effect, use that save and this DC. A utility trap never gains damage by using a higher tier.

**Portable effects.** Damage, save type, profile and theme are drawn from the preset, but the portable version does **not** also apply its dungeon-scale hazard prose. E.g., the Pressure Plate Pit reveals a pit visually but does not permanently alter terrain or drop a creature through a floor; the Summoning Circle does not spawn a combatant; a Teleport Snare does not send someone to another scene; a Wall Flame Jet does not attack everything caught in the animation. Aside from the Practice Snare, only the portable utility rule below and explicit class features add actual effects. A DM can describe a form of effect consistent with the selected schematic, but the art or ordinary preset description does not increase its mechanical reach.

### Level 1: Practice Schematics

These two small designs are available at all levels, including before you choose a discipline. They cost one charge each, use the same limits, and do not gain higher-tier dice.

| Schematic | Save | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Practice Dart | Dexterity | 1d6 Piercing damage, half on a successful save. |
| Practice Snare | Dexterity | On a failure, the creature's Speed is reduced by 10 feet (minimum 0) until the start of your next turn. No damage. |

### Level 2: Read the Mechanism

When you make an Intelligence (Investigation) check to detect or understand a trap, or an ability check using Thieves’ Tools to disarm one, double your Proficiency Bonus for the check if you are already proficient. Proficiency is never doubled twice.

### Level 2: Exploit the Opening

Once per turn **on your turn**, when you hit a creature with a weapon attack, deal an extra 1d4 damage of the weapon’s type if either it is within 5 feet of one of your armed portable traps, or it triggered one of your portable traps since the end of your last turn. This bonus applies only to the attack; it doesn't add damage to a trap or spend a charge. The die becomes 1d6 at Trapwright level 11 and 1d8 at level 17.

### Level 3: Trap Discipline

Choose **Ironwork Engineer**, **Sigil Binder**, **Wildway Warden**, or **Mawkeeper**. A discipline grants features at levels 3, 6, 10, 14 and 18, plus exclusive catalog schematics. You unlock all the schematics in your discipline's available tiers; you don't keep a prepared subset.

| Schematic tier | Trapwright level | Damage on failed save if damaging |
|---|---:|---|
| I | 3 | 2d6 |
| II | 5 | 4d6 |
| III | 11 | 6d6 |
| IV | 17 | 8d6 |

A schematic doesn't increase in tier because you use it at a higher level. If you want stronger damage, use one of your later unlocked designs. Your two practice schematics remain available.

**Utility schematics.** A preset with no damage formula makes a brief utility tile, not a source of damage. If its preset lists no save, it sends you a clear cue that an opponent passed, provided you are within 60 feet; it doesn't commandeer guards or create a countdown. If it lists a save, on failure apply the discipline's utility result below. On success there is no effect. An effect must be consistent with the schematic's fiction, lasts no longer than the start of your next turn unless stated otherwise, and can't create cover, block line of sight, or suppress a creature’s features beyond the stated result.

| Discipline | Portable utility result on a failed save |
|---|---|
| Ironwork Engineer | The creature loses 10 feet of Speed, to a minimum of 0. |
| Sigil Binder | The creature can't take Reactions. |
| Wildway Warden | The trap's 5-foot square is Difficult Terrain for that creature. |
| Mawkeeper | You know the creature’s direction while it is within 30 feet; this does not reveal its exact location or let you see it. |

A discipline damage rider does not apply to its utility traps unless its wording specifically includes them. Outside initiative, treat “until the start of your next turn” as 6 seconds.

### Level 4: Ability Score Improvement

You gain the Ability Score Improvement feat or another feat for which you qualify. You gain this feature again at levels 8, 12 and 16.

### Level 5: Extra Attack

You can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the Attack action on your turn. This doesn't let you Deploy a Trap as an attack until you gain Quick Set.

### Level 7: Clear the Crossing

When a creature you can see within 30 feet is about to attempt a saving throw against a trap, you can take a Reaction to call out a warning. If the creature can hear you, add your Intelligence modifier (minimum +1) to its save. Call the warning before the roll. You can use this feature twice and regain all uses when you finish a Long Rest. It cannot modify a check to disarm a trap or a save against your own portable trap.

### Level 9: Site Survey

By spending 1 minute looking around a contiguous area no more than 20 feet on a side, you can tell the DM you're surveying for concealed hazards. The DM tells you whether visible evidence points to a mechanical, magical, environmental or living hazard in that area, and gives you one usable clue if there is evidence. This doesn't see through walls, reveal a tile or its DC, disarm anything, or replace checks needed to confirm its location or function. Once per Short or Long Rest, you can perform the survey as an Action instead.

### Level 13: Quick Set

When you take the Attack action on your turn, you can replace one of your attacks with Deploy Trap. It still costs a charge and follows all limit, distance, target and placement rules. This isn't a second placement on the same turn. Allow the normal trap feature in Foundry as a substitute for an attack only when your group agrees to use Quick Set.

### Level 15: Safety Reflex

When a trap that isn't yours allows you a Dexterity saving throw to take half damage, take no damage on a success and only half on a failure instead. You can't use this benefit while Incapacitated. It doesn't prevent associated effects unless the trap's own rules say a successful save does.

### Level 19: Epic Boon

You gain an Epic Boon feat or another feat for which you qualify.

### Level 20: Last Reserve

When you roll Initiative with no Trap Charges remaining, you regain two charges. Once you regain charges this way, you can't do so again until you finish a Long Rest. In addition, when you take an Action to dismantle one of your own armed traps within 5 feet without triggering it, you may preserve it as one Trap Charge, up to your maximum. You can do so once per Long Rest. Dismantled traps go away before you regain a charge.

## Ironwork Engineer
**Mechanical control • 22 schematics.** You direct the close-quarters fight: once a floor plate goes, an enemy has to fight from where it lands.

### Level 3: Hold the Line

Gain proficiency with Smith’s Tools or Mason’s Tools, your choice; if you are already proficient in both, choose another artisan’s tool. A creature that fails a save against a damaging Ironwork schematic loses 10 feet of Speed, to a minimum of 0, until the start of your next turn. This reduction doesn't stack with itself.
### Level 6: Jack and Slide

As a Bonus Action, reposition one of your still-armed Ironwork tiles up to 5 feet into an unoccupied square you could deploy to. You must be within 5 feet of both the old and new square. It doesn't trigger when moved, doesn't cost a charge, and keeps its expiry time. You can use this feature twice per Long Rest. The DM moves the tile if needed.
### Level 10: Locking Brace

You can use a Bonus Action to brace yourself, lasting until the start of your next turn or until you move voluntarily. While braced, you have Advantage on saves or checks against being knocked Prone or moved against your will. After using it, you can’t do so again until the start of your next turn. Armor or an occupied hand does not alter how it works.
### Level 14: Impact Stop

When you or a creature you can see within 15 feet would be pushed or pulled, use your Reaction to shorten that movement by up to 10 feet. If this stops that movement completely, the target also avoids being knocked Prone by that same push or pull. You can use this Reaction a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once) per Long Rest.
### Level 18: Hard Lock

When a Large or smaller creature fails a save against one of your damaging Ironwork traps, you can apply Restrained until the start of your next turn instead of Hold the Line. No extra save. Once you do so, you can’t use Hard Lock again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

## Sigil Binder
**Arcane wards • 22 schematics.** You specialise in bursts, sealing reactions and tuning wards to enemies’ defenses.

### Level 3: Seal the Response

Gain proficiency in Arcana. If you have it already, gain proficiency in one Trapwright skill you lack. A creature that fails a save against a damaging Sigil Binder schematic can't take Reactions until the start of your next turn.
### Level 6: Overwrite

When you deploy a damaging Sigil Binder schematic that normally deals Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning or Thunder damage, you can change its damage to any other type on that list. Its saving throw and tier don't change. Use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once) per Long Rest. Use the default type unless the DM adjusts the placed tile.
### Level 10: Countercharge

When you take Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, Necrotic, Psychic, Radiant or Thunder damage, you can take a Reaction to gain Resistance to that instance of damage. You must be within 10 feet of one of your armed Sigil Binder traps, and you do not expend or trigger it. Recover this use on a Short or Long Rest.
### Level 14: Cut the Channel

Once per turn, after a creature fails a save against a damaging Sigil Binder trap, you can cause that roll to ignore the target's Resistance to the trap’s damage type. It never ignores Immunity. You can use this feature twice per Long Rest; declare it before damage is applied.
### Level 18: Lingering Seal

When a creature **succeeds** on a save against one of your damaging Sigil Binder traps, you can still bar its Reactions until the start of your next turn, as if it had failed for that rider only. Its damage and other effects remain those of a success. You can do this a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once) per Long Rest.

## Wildway Warden
**Environmental hazards • 21 schematics.** You channel terrain itself into a short-lived advantage. Your hazards guide enemies into bad ground and give allies better routes.

### Level 3: Bad Ground

Gain proficiency in Nature or Survival. On a failed save against a damaging Wildway trap, its square and one unoccupied adjacent square you choose become Difficult Terrain until the start of your next turn. A creature passing through this terrain doesn't trigger it again, and overlapping areas from Bad Ground don't increase the movement cost. Indicate the second square to the DM. It's temporary rough ground, not a wall or a new trap.
### Level 6: Sure Route

Natural nonmagical Difficult Terrain doesn't cost you extra movement, and you have a Climb Speed and a Swim Speed equal to your Speed. This doesn't grant the ability to breathe underwater or protect you from harmful terrain effects.
### Level 10: Party Passage

You and creatures you designate when you place a Wildway trap ignore Difficult Terrain created by your Bad Ground. You can designate up to your Proficiency Bonus in creatures per placement with no additional action. This doesn't exempt anyone from unrelated natural or magical terrain.
### Level 14: Crosscurrent

Once per turn, when a Large or smaller creature fails a save against a damaging Wildway trap, you can move it up to 5 feet horizontally to an unoccupied supported square that you can see within 5 feet of it. This is involuntary movement; it can't cause another portable trap to trigger. You can use Crosscurrent twice per Short or Long Rest.
### Level 18: Spillover

When a damaging Wildway trap triggers, you can select one other opposing creature you can see within 5 feet of the triggering square. It makes the same saving throw against your trap DC, taking 3d6 damage of the trap's type on a failure or half on a success. It suffers no failure rider, doesn't count as another crossing and doesn't set off anything else. Once you use Spillover, you can't use it again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

## Mawkeeper
**Living traps and ambushes • 21 schematics.** You train the room to bite. As your devices take on disguises and personalities, you learn to make a strike count and slip away before anyone realises where it came from.

### Level 3: Marked as Prey

Gain proficiency in Stealth or Deception. When a creature fails a save against a damaging Mawkeeper trap, you have Advantage on the next weapon attack roll you make against it before the end of your next turn. The benefit is used even if you miss and doesn't stack with itself.
### Level 6: Vanish from the Bait

Once on your turn, immediately after you successfully deploy a Mawkeeper trap, you can move up to 10 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks. Spend that movement from your available Speed; it isn't additional movement. It doesn't let you move while your Speed is 0, pass through creatures or objects, or bypass Difficult Terrain.
### Level 10: The Maw Remembers

When a creature fails a save against a damaging Mawkeeper trap, you can sense its rough direction if it's on the same plane and within 60 feet of you, for up to 1 minute. This is not sight, exact location, illumination, or a way to ignore the Invisible condition. You can track only one creature at a time; marking another ends the previous sense.
### Level 14: Borrowed Bite

When a creature you can see within 30 feet fails a save against your damaging Mawkeeper trap, you can use your Reaction to add 2d6 damage of the trap’s type to the failed-save damage. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once) per Long Rest. It doesn't apply to damage dealt to a second creature.
### Level 18: Predatory Entrance

When a creature fails its save against your damaging Mawkeeper trap and lies within 60 feet of you, you can use your Reaction to teleport to a supported unoccupied space you can see within 5 feet of it and then make one melee weapon attack against it. This movement doesn’t trigger portable traps. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

## Foundry play and adjudication

Use the Trapwright sheet action or player placement control included with your module build. Own and select your character’s token. Choose an unlocked schematic and the adjacent square. The active GM client validates placement and creates the tile on behalf of the player; player character ownership is not GM permission. The chosen sound and animation play on reveal, the damage roll and save are posted in chat, and the DM applies HP, conditions, and subclass riders. If the table doesn't have an active GM client, tell the DM the intended schematic and location; don't run a GM script or retry a request that might have succeeded without first checking the map.

The module handles ordinary deployment, charge spending, damage rolls, save prompts, the portable utility result in chat, and schematic access. It tracks the one-hour limit in Foundry world time and handles the normal rest reset with an active GM. The DM handles applying HP and conditions, class and discipline riders beyond the core result, action economy, and tandem movement. If the clock isn't advanced, the character dies, or another module doesn't identify a forced move, the DM may need to clear the tile or disregard a trigger manually. Last Reserve's charge recovery is a separate tabletop feature; record just the recovered charges, not a full Long Rest reset. Tabletop timing and movement rules still apply.

**Player map tip:** Look for a route an opponent would choose naturally. Tell your party what you're trying to do rather than asking them to spend their turns forcibly moving a target into a trap. On a turn when nothing will cross, use Extra Attack or support an ally instead of spending a charge.

**DM guidance.** Existing GM trap tiles and portable Trapwright tiles are distinct. The class doesn't confer control over scene traps, set them for free, duplicate them, or replace their DCs. Many original presets describe large cinematic hazards that the DM can still place normally. Portable editions intentionally shrink their effect without substituting new maps or spawning monsters. For spotting/disarming a portable trap where it matters, use the trap DC for a sensible search or tools check and allow creatures to detour or engage it from range as the situation warrants.

**Playtest targets.** Measure triggered traps per charge, not just successful attacks on a stationary target. Compare open rooms versus corridors, two versus six encounter days, party synergy, and encounters with flying or intelligent enemies. Tier IV has a ceiling of 28 average damage on a single failed save before features, is limited to one target and one charge, and competes for turn economy as well as space; that is a design target rather than a proven balance claim.
